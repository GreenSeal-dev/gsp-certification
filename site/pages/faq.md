# Frequently asked questions

## What is GSP?

Green Software Practices (GSP) is an open standard: a set of practices for building energy- and resource-efficient software and AI systems, maintained by GreenSeal.dev. Organisations can be certified against it through a certification scheme operated by GreenSeal.dev. See the [practices]({{ root }}/practices/) and [certification]({{ root }}/certification/).

## Is GSP open? Can I use it?

Yes. The practices are licensed under CC BY 4.0, so you can use, share and adapt them, including commercially, with attribution. Certification is not covered by the licence. See [Licence and certification]({{ root }}/about/).

## Is GSP a certification or an assessment?

Both. GSP certification is based on a structured assessment. Self-assessed certification is based on self-declared information. Verified and Verified+ certifications are awarded only after independent verification by GreenSeal.dev.

## Is this a product certification?

No. GSP certifies **organisational practices**, not individual software products.

## How is GSP different from other sustainability certifications and standards?

GSP focuses on the development practices that make software more resource-efficient. Most sustainability frameworks overlook how software products themselves are built and run.

## How does GSP compare with ISO 14001?

ISO 14001 is a standard for environmental management systems. It addresses an organisation's environmental impact and supports continuous improvement, but it overlooks the impact of running software and the reductions that energy-efficient software design can achieve.

## Does paying for verification guarantee certification?

No. Organisations pay for an independent verification process, not for a badge. The Verified and Verified+ badges are awarded only if the criteria are met.

Every paid verification results in a detailed technical assessment report, whatever the outcome.

## What happens if we do not pass Verified certification?

If the criteria are not fully met:

- your existing certification status (for example, Self-assessed) remains unchanged
- you receive a detailed report identifying gaps and areas to improve
- you can address the gaps and request re-verification later

## Can an organisation enrol in Verified+ without already being certified?

Yes. Organisations can join the Verified+ engagement even if they don't yet meet all the criteria. The Verified+ badge is awarded only once the criteria are met.

## Does GSP measure carbon emissions?

GSP focuses on **engineering practices and measurement capabilities**. Carbon metrics may be included when available, but they are not mandatory.

## Is the badge time-limited?

Yes. Every certification is valid for a limited period and must be renewed to stay active. See [Certification]({{ root }}/certification/) for the validity of each status.

## Can start-ups apply?

Yes. GSP is designed for organisations of any size.

## What is the difference between Self-assessed and Verified certification?

They are not the same checklist with different scrutiny. Self-assessed certification currently uses its own, lighter questionnaire and relies entirely on what the organisation declares. Verified certification covers all {{ level2_count }} Level 2 practices and adds an independent review of the evidence by GreenSeal.dev.

## Can we make public sustainability claims using GSP?

- **Self-assessed:** not suitable for strong external claims.
- **Verified and Verified+:** suitable for public sustainability and ESG communication.

Organisations are responsible for making sure their claims are accurate and match their certification status.

## Who can submit a GSP self-assessment?

Someone authorised to represent the organisation and confirm that the information provided is accurate.

## Who operates the certification?

GreenSeal, Lda. (GreenSeal.dev), an independent company specialised in sustainable software and AI.

## Can we lose the certification?

Yes. Misuse of the badge or misleading claims can lead to revocation. See the [Terms of Certification]({{ root }}/terms/).

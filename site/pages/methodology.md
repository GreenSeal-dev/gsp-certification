# Methodology

GSP assesses whether sustainable software and AI practices are **systematically adopted** within an organisation. It looks at practices, not marketing claims.

## Evidence-based

Each practice states what an organisation shall do and gives examples of evidence that can show it is in place. Depending on the certification status, the evidence includes:

- documented processes
- technical artefacts
- measurement results

Self-assessed certification relies on what the organisation declares. Verified certification adds an independent review of the evidence by GreenSeal.dev. See [Certification]({{ root }}/certification/).

## Research foundation

The practices are derived from peer-reviewed research in Sustainable Software Engineering and Green AI, not from proprietary frameworks or internal opinion. The work behind them covers:

- Sustainable Software Engineering
- Green AI
- Performance and energy profiling
- Organisational knowledge transfer

The methodology is informed by work published at venues including ICSE, CAIN, WIREs and ACM SIGSOFT. [Read about the research behind GreenSeal.dev](https://www.greenseal.dev/about/).

The Level 2 practices have been reviewed against the six lifecycle phases of [ISO/IEC TS 20125-1:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/86105.html). The practices that apply at Level 2 only are the ones added in that review.

## Principles

- **Engineering first, not offsets.** GSP is about how software is designed, built and run, not about compensating for its emissions.
- **Evidence-driven and auditable.** Every practice says what evidence can show it is in place.
- **Open tooling.** Energy measurements use open-source tools such as [EnergiBridge](https://github.com/tdurieux/EnergiBridge), not a proprietary black box.
- **Software and AI.** The practices apply to software-intensive systems, cloud-native applications and AI/ML workloads.
- **Built for real teams.** GSP is designed for engineering organisations of any size, from scale-ups to enterprises.
